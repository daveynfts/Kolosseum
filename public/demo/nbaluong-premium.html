Credibility signals

Method and confidence standard

This assessment separates four evidence types:

  • First-hand observations: statements presented as the author’s own experience, such as using the simulator, obtaining referrals, completing information requests, attending an event, or receiving a reward.
  • Opinions: judgments such as “I remain bullish,” “the exchange is reputable,” or “users should stay calm.”
  • Predictions: forward-looking statements, including expectations that rewards would arrive soon or that blockchain users would eventually move to Vietnamese exchanges.
  • Third-party claims: information attributed to SCEX moderators, leaderboards, campaign rules, prize schedules, tax procedures, or event operations but not independently verified in the supplied snapshot.

Hypotheses versus findings

A reasonable hypothesis is that the author participated in a SCEX-centered community or referral campaign where users discussed rankings, rewards, and future trading activity. The repeated code, the prize structure, and the cluster of replies are consistent with that hypothesis.

It would be inappropriate to upgrade that hypothesis into a finding that the participants were coordinated, that the engagement was inauthentic, or that the account was part of an organized promotional network. Engagement counters alone do not establish bots, paid amplification, sockpuppets, or a relationship graph. High reply counts can arise naturally in a campaign with active participants, particularly when the author is posting instructions and responding to questions.

Data required to validate a network conclusion

A defensible network analysis would require:

  1. The original parent-post IDs for each reply and the full text of the parent posts.
  2. Bidirectional interaction data: replies, reposts, quotes, mentions, and timestamps for both sides.
  3. Account-level metadata for the named handles, including account age, historical SCEX content, audience overlap, and recurring co-posting patterns.
  4. Referral attribution records showing which code or URL generated each sign-up and whether the author received benefits.
  5. Official SCEX campaign records identifying ambassadors, affiliates, KOLs, moderators, prize winners, and payment batches.
  6. A normalized exposure dataset separating organic views from repost-driven or paid distribution.
  7. Full account history outside the supplied window, including deleted, quoted, and media posts.
